01 / JUSPAY · 2024–Present

Redesigning Payment Checkout — From Anxiety to Confidence

Fintech Mobile UX Checkout Flow UX Research
→
Challenge
Payment drop-offs during checkout were 34% above industry average, particularly on the UPI and card selection steps.
My Role
Lead Product Designer — end to end, from research to shipped
Timeline
14 weeks
2024
Platform
Android & iOS
SDK Integration

Context & Problem

JUSPAY powers the checkout layer for major Indian e-commerce platforms — meaning every friction point in our interface directly affects merchant revenue. My mandate was to investigate why users were abandoning payment flows mid-way, and to systematically redesign the experience.

"Users weren't confused about what to do — they were anxious about whether it would work."

Research Finding · User Interview, Session 07

The existing checkout had evolved over years of feature additions without a cohesive UX strategy. The result: a fragmented flow that felt different depending on which merchant deployed it, with inconsistent feedback states and unclear error recovery.

Research & Discovery

I conducted 24 contextual interviews across 3 cities, focusing on tier-2 users who represent 60% of India's growing UPI user base. I paired this with funnel analytics to identify the exact drop-off moments.

🔍 Insight 01 — Trust Gap
Users trusted their UPI app, not the merchant checkout. Any redirection or pop-up felt suspicious. 71% users who saw a "Verifying…" loader with no time estimate abandoned the flow.
🔍 Insight 02 — Decision Paralysis
Showing 8+ payment methods simultaneously increased cognitive load. Users spent an avg of 14 seconds choosing — and 28% selected incorrectly on first attempt.
🔍 Insight 03 — Error Recovery
When UPI failed (common with bank server timeouts), 68% of users did not know how to retry or switch methods. They simply exited the app.
🔍 Insight 04 — Confirmation Anxiety
Users pressed the "Pay" button and immediately felt uncertain. The lack of clear pending → processing → success states created a "did it go through?" anxiety that led to duplicate taps.

Key Design Solutions

The redesign focused on three core principles: Progressive Disclosure (surface only what's needed, when needed), Confident Feedback (every state change acknowledged instantly), and Graceful Recovery (failure is a feature, not an afterthought).

02 / BETTERPLACE · 2022–2024

Oust — Making Upskilling Feel Human for Frontline Workers

EdTech Mobile-First Accessibility B2B2C
→
Challenge
Mandatory corporate training was being skipped by 60%+ of blue-collar workers because the app felt designed for office workers, not for them.
My Role
Associate Product Designer — solo on mobile, collaborated with 2 senior designers
Timeline
6 months
2023
Users
Frontline workers, Security, Housekeeping, Retail

The Real Problem Behind the Problem

At BetterPlace, we initially framed this as an "engagement" problem — low module completion rates. But contextual research revealed something deeper: the workers didn't lack motivation. They lacked relevance, trust, and a design that respected their lives.

"I open the app and it feels like it's for someone else. The English words, the time it takes — I'm on a shift break. I can't do this."

User Interview · Security Guard, Bengaluru (translated from Kannada)

This single insight reframed everything. The problem was not engagement mechanics — it was empathy. We needed to design for someone on a 15-minute break, with a 3G connection, who might be semi-literate in English, and who has never considered "learning" a leisure activity.

Field Research

I spent 3 days shadowing workers across job sites — a commercial complex, a retail store floor, and a hospital housekeeping team. This was non-negotiable. Data alone would never reveal what watching someone use an app during a 10-minute chai break reveals.

📍 Context: Usage Moments
Workers used their phones primarily during breaks (10–20 mins), often in noisy environments. They used headphones rarely. Text-heavy screens were scrolled past without reading.
🌐 Language Barrier
60% of test users struggled with English-only content. Vocabulary like "Module", "Certification", "Assessment" required explanation. They felt judged when they couldn't answer questions.
🏆 Motivation: Recognition
The strongest motivator was not incentives — it was visibility. "If my supervisor could see I completed training, I'd do it." Social proof and manager visibility drove completion more than badges.
📱 Device Constraints
Low-end Android devices (2–3GB RAM) on 3G/4G. Heavy animations caused lag. Large images failed to load. Battery anxiety was real — users avoided apps they felt "used too much battery."

Design Principles We Committed To

✦
Scannable First
Every screen readable in under 5 seconds. Icons carry meaning. Text is a supplement, not the primary medium.
✦
Low Data
No autoplay. Offline capability for started modules. Image compression mandated. Lightweight animations only.
✦
Visible Progress
Every action acknowledged. Streaks, supervisor visibility, and peer ranking surfaced prominently without being condescending.
✦
Respectful Tone
Language simplified and humanised. No "assessment" — "Quick Check". No "module" — "Today's Lesson". Failure framed as "Try Again", never shown as a score.
